## CLM-701 (SRC-141)

**Patient-facing claim:** In a small randomized, open-label study of 56 people with ulcerative colitis in remission who had iron-deficiency anaemia, the researchers did not detect a statistically significant difference in haemoglobin, red-cell indices or iron measures between daily and alternate-day oral iron after 8 weeks, and adverse events and flares were also not significantly different between the groups. This does not prove that the two schedules are equivalent, and it is not a recommendation to change iron dosing: talk to a clinician before changing how you take iron.

**Exact excerpt:** "Our study did not show any significant difference in mean hemoglobin levels, RBC indices, or iron profiles between the daily and alternate-day oral iron groups. Adverse events and disease flare were also not statistically significant in the two groups."

**Applicability:** ulcerative_colitis | **Evidence:** randomized controlled trial | **Confidence:** low | **Review status:** owner_reviewed_for_pilot

**Limitations:** 56 participants, open-label, 8 weeks, one oral iron formulation, single country; the authors say larger studies are needed. 'No significant difference' does not prove the two schedules are equivalent.

**Citation:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.118014
